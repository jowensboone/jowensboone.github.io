--- layout: single title: "Research" permalink: /research/ author_profile: true --- My research sits at the intersection of race, the legal system, and psychophysiology. I'm interested in how physiological and cognitive processes shape public judgments about policing and legal legitimacy, and more broadly, how people experience, perceive, and respond to race-related discrimination. ---
### Vicarious and Direct Discrimination I study how people process and respond to discrimination they experience directly versus discrimination they witness happen to others, and how these different pathways shape downstream attitudes, emotional responses, and behavior. ### Race, Policing, and Psychophysiology My work examines how physiological and cognitive processes — including psychophysiological measures — shape public judgments about police use of force and legitimacy, with a particular focus on how racial context influences those judgments.
### Collective Action I'm interested in the psychological processes that motivate people to engage in collective action in response to perceived injustice, particularly in the context of policing and racial inequity. I am also interested in how collective action and vicarious discrimination experiences interact. ### Race Perception and Categorization More broadly, I'm interested in how people perceive and categorize race, and how these foundational processes intersect with judgments about the legal system.
